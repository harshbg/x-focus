X Focus

X as a small Android app: grey, no video, no ads, and a countdown of today's minutes at the top of the screen.

Install the Android app

  1. On your phone, open the Claude chat this came from and download x-focus.apk.
  2. Tap the downloaded file. Android asks whether this source may install apps: allow it. If Play Protect says it does not recognise the developer, choose to install anyway.
  3. Open X Focus and sign in with your X username and password. The "Sign in with Google" and "Sign in with Apple" buttons do not work inside the app.
  4. Pick your daily limit when it asks. That is the whole setup.

Already have an earlier version? Install the new file over it. You stay signed in and keep today's clock.

Remove the X app so links stop opening the full-colour version. To send x.com links to X Focus, open Android Settings, Apps, X Focus, Open by default, and add the x.com links.

The app is a single screen showing x.com through Android's built-in web view, which is the same engine as Chrome. Links that leave X open in your normal browser.

What happens when you use it

The clock runs only while X is on screen
The digits and the blocks under them are blue for the first third of your limit, yellow for the middle third and red for the last. One block is ten minutes. It resets at midnight.
Opening X takes three seconds
A black screen counts down from 3 and asks what you are here for. "Not now" closes X again. It comes back whenever you have been away for more than a few seconds.
Every five minutes the screen goes black for a minute
It tells you how many minutes are gone and asks whether this is still useful. "Keep going" unlocks after 60 seconds. Your clock is paused while you wait. "I'm done" closes X.
At zero, X is locked until tomorrow
You can take 5 more minutes, but only after a 30-second wait that grows each time you do it.
Video and ads become a small dotted box
"Video hidden" keeps the post's text. "Ad hidden" shows nothing of the ad at all. The app also refuses to download video.
Hold a picture to see that post in colour
You get 5 minutes of colour a day. Its own clock appears in the timer bar and runs only while colour is on. Tap it to stop, or scroll the post away. Opening the photo full-screen keeps it in colour.
Three buttons at the bottom
Home, Following and your first pinned list replace X's bottom bar and the scrolling tabs at the top. Pin your list in X first; its button takes the list's name. Search, Notifications (with the unread count) and Chat are at the top of the settings screen.
If something on X looks wrong
Go to the screen that looks wrong, tap the timer, and choose Copy page details for Claude. Paste the result into the chat. It describes the layout of the page so the next update can fix it.
Less clutter
Spaces, Grok and the Premium upsells are gone. A Space in a post becomes a dotted "Space hidden" box.
A weekly summary
Once every seven days it shows your minutes for each day, the total, and how often you took extra time. The last seven days are also at the bottom of the settings screen.
Tap the timer for settings
A slider sets the limit from 5 to 90 minutes. Lowering applies at once; raising applies from the next day. Colour can be 5 or 10 minutes, or off, and each of the items above has its own switch.

Updates

Once a day the app looks on GitHub for a newer version of its script and switches to it the next time you come back to the app. To get one straight away, tap the timer and choose Check for updates. There is nothing to reinstall. The version number is at the bottom of the settings screen.

Without the app: Firefox

If you would sooner not install an APK, the same script runs in Firefox for Android. Chrome on Android cannot load scripts or extensions, which is why the app exists.

  1. Install Firefox from the Play Store.
  2. In Firefox, open the menu, go to Extensions, and add Violentmonkey. Tampermonkey works too.
  3. Tap Copy the script below.
  4. Open Violentmonkey from the Extensions menu, open its dashboard, tap + and choose New. Delete what is in the editor, paste, and save.
  5. Go to x.com in Firefox and sign in. Pick your daily limit when it asks. That is the whole setup.
  6. From the Firefox menu choose Add to Home screen to get an icon that opens it like an app.

iPhone

The same script should work in Safari with the free Userscripts app from the App Store. Turn the extension on in Safari's settings, pick a folder for it in the app, and save the script from the Firefox section above into that folder as x-focus.user.js. This path has not been tried on a real iPhone yet.
